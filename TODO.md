**GKDV research program (TF-15..TF-19).** TF-15 through TF-19 form a coherent research arc towards the **GK Deterrent Value** metric — a per-frame measure of how much the defending goalkeeper's actual position depresses opponent attempt-probabilities (shot / cross / key pass) relative to a league-average "ghost" GK in the same frame state. Origin: 2026-05-01 deterrent-effect investigation; closes the published-literature gap that no GK-evaluation framework today (StatsBomb four-pillar, Lamberts GVM, Sloan SAC data-driven framework, Anzer & Bauer xGOT, Driblab Goals Prevented) measures positioning-as-deterrent. **Foundation:** TF-7 (pitch control) + TF-13 (frame-based GK ID) + TF-14 (defensive-line geometry). **Layer 1:** TF-15 (threat-weighted GK influence primitives; shipped 3.10.0). **Layer 2:** TF-16 / TF-17 / TF-18 (decision-probability surfaces for shot-occurrence and cross-attempt + ghost-GK regression). **Layer 3:** TF-19 (composition + validation harness). **Sequencing:** TF-15 lands first as a self-contained publishable primitive; TF-16/17/18 can be parallelised (TF-18's regression baseline is the most spec-complete of the trio, TF-17's GK-confounder extension is the most research-heavy); TF-19 is two PR cycles and gets its own ADR. **External complements:** DAS (Bischofberger & Baca 2026, `accessible-space` on PyPI; TF-28, shipped 3.8.0) provides physics-based counterfactual space valuation; cover shadows (Cascioli et al. 2025; TF-30, shipped 3.11.0) provide lane-specific pass-obstruction metrics. Both can serve as independent cross-checks on the probability-surface decomposition in TF-19.

**TF-33 (Dunkin'):** Off-ball xT — per-player per-frame positional value via `xT(x,y) × pitch_control(x,y)`. **Trivial composition of shipped primitives.** Both building blocks already exist: `ExpectedThreat` (`xthreat.py`) provides the xT grid, pitch control (TF-7, `tracking/pitch_control/`) provides the spatial control surface. The composition is ~20–30 lines: for each player frame, look up xT at (x,y), multiply by that player's pitch-control share at (x,y). Per-frame output: `off_ball_xt` float column on frames. Action-coupled wrapper `add_off_ball_xt(actions, frames, home_team_id)` emitting per-team aggregated off-ball xT at each action's linked frame. Per-player per-match aggregation (total/avg) stays consumer-side — that's analytics-layer, not feature-layer. **Academic reference:** Soccermatics Lesson 7 (Sumpter); composes Spearman 2017 pitch control + Karun Singh 2018 xT. **Priority:** Low — bundle with next PR touching pitch-control subpackage rather than dedicating a PR. Origin: 2026-05-08 lakehouse TF-10 planning review.

**TF-35 (Wicked):** Off-ball run type classification — overlap / underlap / far side / advance / support taxonomy. **Priority-deferred; no published academic anchor.** TF-4 (shipped 3.9.0) detects off-ball runners and emits `n_off_ball_runners_toward_goal_pre_window` as a scalar count. A richer classification of *how* teammates run (overlap on the outside, underlap inside, advance beyond the ball, support behind it, far-side weak-side runs) would enable per-type VAEP features and scouting-layer aggregations. **Reference:** Gradient Sports blog, "Measuring Off-Ball Movement Using Broadcast Tracking & Event Data" (Schram, 2026-05-06) — describes a hierarchical run-type classification + 15-control-point spatial normalization + additive modifiers (in behind, into box, distance band, ball receipt). Commercial product description, not peer-reviewed; Gradient's zone definitions are proprietary and would need independent specification. The 15-control-point normalization (duration-invariant spatial profile) is a clean engineering pattern worth adopting if this ships. **Implementation sketch:** extend `_off_ball_runs.py` with a `run_type` categorical column per runner; requires ball-carrier-relative lateral position (inside/outside) + lapping-point detection for overlap/underlap; pitch zone definitions for wing/halfspace/centre. ~200–300 LOC on top of TF-4. **Demand signal:** overlap/underlap/advance/support are standard coaching vocabulary used daily in training and match review — quantifying them bridges the analytics-coaching communication gap directly. Value is higher than pure analytics novelty suggests; any consumer building tools for coaching staff would benefit. **Revisit if/when:** a published paper formalises the taxonomy with reproducible definitions, or consumer demand materialises for coaching-vocabulary-aligned run metrics. Origin: 2026-05-13 Gradient Sports blog investigation.

**TF-36 (Dunkin'):** Per-player influence primitives — generalize TF-15 (b) uniquely reachable area to all field players. **Actionable composition of shipped primitives.** TF-15 (`_gk_influence.py`, shipped 3.10.0) computes three GK-specific primitives; two of the three generalize to field players with minimal effort. **(a) Threat-weighted pitch control share** is already available for any player via `compute_pitch_control(decompose=True).player_surface(player_id)` + xT weighting (~15 lines of consumer composition, no new code needed). **(b) Uniquely reachable area** — area a player can reach within τ seconds that no teammate can — generalizes by replacing "back-line defenders" with "all teammates except player_i" and using role-parameterized kinematic constants (reaction_time, max_acceleration) instead of GK-specific values. ~50 LOC. The metric answers "how much dangerous space does only this player cover?" — a direct measure of defensive responsibility, isolation, and positional quality per defender. **(c) Zone closing times** remain GK-specific (six-yard box, near/far post zones have no field-player equivalent without a per-role zone taxonomy). **Scope:** ship a `compute_player_influence(frame, player_id, xt, ...)` returning PC share + uniquely reachable area for any player. Zone closing times stay in `compute_gk_influence`. Needs per-role kinematic constant table (outfield reaction_time ~0.7s, max_accel ~5-6 m/s² vs GK 0.4s / 7.0 m/s²; position-group refinements are a calibration follow-up). **Priority:** Low — bundle with next PR touching `_gk_influence.py` or pitch-control subpackage. Origin: 2026-05-13 Stöckl et al. 2021 investigation → field-player generalization question.

**TF-37 (Wicked):** Pose-estimation features for xG, VAEP, and GKDV. **Data-availability-blocked; high value when unblocked.** Body keypoint data (trunk angle, knee flexion, foot orientation at ball contact) from pose estimation enables three concrete value-adds: **(a) Player-adjusted xG** — current xG treats all shooters as interchangeable; pose features at shot moment make xG per-shooter per-shot. Slots into lakehouse xG-v2 as additional input features. Hirn et al. (FAU Erlangen-Nürnberg, 2026; LISS SAC poster, paper forthcoming) demonstrated improved Brier score on 101 controlled-drill shots using tree-based models with pose features vs. positional-only baselines. **(b) GK pose at shot moment** — GK body orientation, set position, weight distribution at shot time is a stronger signal than centroid position alone. Feeds directly into GKDV research program (TF-15..TF-19); a GK leaning left vs. set square has different save probability regardless of (x, y). **(c) VAEP pose features** — body orientation at action time (not just shots) could inform pass technique quality, dribble body lean, aerial duel posture. New `_frame_aware` xfn category in `tracking.features`. **Blocker:** no tracking provider silly-kicks currently supports (Sportec, Metrica, SkillCorner, Gradient Sports, kloppy gateway) ships body keypoint data. When a provider makes pose features available, the silly-kicks schema (`TRACKING_FRAMES_COLUMNS`) would need per-player-per-frame keypoint columns or a linked keypoint DataFrame. **Hirn et al. limitations:** 9-participant controlled drill (not match data), right-foot only, self-acknowledged need for real-game validation. Watch for the published paper. **Revisit if/when:** a tracking provider supported by silly-kicks ships body keypoint features, or a new provider with pose data is added via kloppy. Origin: 2026-05-13 LISS SAC poster investigation.

**TF-34 (Wicked):** ELASTIC event-tracking synchronization as `link_actions_to_frames(method="elastic")`. **Priority-deferred; reference exists.** Kim et al. 2025 ([arXiv:2508.09238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09238), MLSA 2025; [GitHub](https://github.com/hyunsungkim-ds/elastic)) propose a tracking-only synchronization framework achieving 88.4% exact alignment vs ETSY's 21.1% ([Van Roy, Cascioli & Davis 2023](https://github.com/ML-KULeuven/ETSY)). Uses ball acceleration features, type-specific rules, separate major/minor event detection, and pass end-time detection — ~500+ LOC of event-type-specific logic, not a simple `method=` swap. Current `link_actions_to_frames` uses `pd.merge_asof` nearest-timestamp within 0.2s tolerance, which is adequate when both data sources have reasonable timestamp quality (Sportec natively synchronized, StatsBomb 360, Metrica, Gradient Sports). ELASTIC solves the harder problem of synchronizing disparate datasets where event timestamps drift 0.5–2s (primarily DFL/IDSSE). Consumers needing ELASTIC-quality sync can use the published package directly. **Revisit if/when:** consumer demand materializes for kloppy-free ingestion (TF-23) where event-tracking alignment becomes a bottleneck, or empirical testing shows current 0.2s tolerance degrades downstream feature quality for a specific provider. Origin: 2026-05-08 lakehouse TF-10 planning review.
