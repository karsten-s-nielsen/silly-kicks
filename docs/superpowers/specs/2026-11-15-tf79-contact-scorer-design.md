## 1. Why

### 1.1 The rules missed (r1)

The detector v2 rules missed the bar on the development half (D§6 run on commit 4 `9b9e8838`, 24 matches, 30,189 pairs). No grid point was feasible; the best "right" was 0.345 (radius 1.5 m, dv 1 m/s; false first 0.200). The rule grid on `cb456270` repeats the result (`chosen` = none).

### 1.2 The v1 scorer missed (r7)

The v1 driver's development run on the clean commit 4 `cb456270` (24 matches, 30,189 pairs; the staged-equals-served check passed on 64 of 64 units) found no feasible point. Its best out-of-fold "right" was 0.335 at threshold 0.30 (false first 0.278, missed 0.344, timing −0.067 s). False first ≤ 0.10 needs a threshold of 0.45 or more, where "right" is 0.229 or less. Per section 3 the held-out half was not scored. The run is reproducible from the bundle of `cb456270` (status line).

### 1.3 Diagnosis (read-only, the development half only, DGX, 2026-11-16)

The scripts and their outputs are kept off the repo (`D:\Temp\cross-def\scorer_diag*.py`, `inspect_tracks.py`, `track_ceiling.py`, `window_sweep.py`, `supported_pairs.py`, `proto_v3.py`; DGX `~/cross-def/`). Each asserts that it reads no held-out game.

- **The model class is not the limit.** On the v1 features, a gradient-boosted model (scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingClassifier`) reaches "right" 0.390 at best and 0.347 at false first ≤ 0.10 (out-of-fold AUC 0.928, against 0.898 for the logistic model).
- **The truth and the tracks disagree on many pairs.** Within ±τ (0.2 s) of PFF's reception, the tracks put PFF's receiver within 1.5 m of the ball on 58.6 % of pairs (2.0 m: 65.4 %; ±1.0 s and 3.0 m: 76.4 %). On the pairs the v1 scorer classed `wrong_player`, PFF's receiver is a median 15 m from the ball. So "right ≥ 0.60" over all pairs is at or above what the tracks can show.
- **The losses on the supported pairs are soft receptions and early credits.** On the missed pairs, 63–68 % had the receiver as an option scored below the threshold. Of the false firsts, 51–63 % credit the right receiver 0.3–0.4 s (median) before PFF's start: the ball arrives at the receiver before PFF marks control.
- **The ball channel is handled.** The GS `balls` channel is sample-and-hold (14.985 Hz positions emitted twice at 29.97 Hz; 50 % zero moves); the ADR-125 dedup removes the held samples before differencing. A wider velocity window does not help: with the prototype of the 2.3 design (`window_sweep.py`, the default HGB settings with early stopping, control features from the staged velocity columns), `kinematic_window_s` 0.067/0.133/0.2/0.267 s gives all-pairs "right" at false first ≤ 0.10 of 0.417/0.416/0.414/0.392.
- **Measure levers are small on all pairs.** An early-side τ of 0.5 s adds 0.04–0.08 "right"; excluding pairs with a gap over 5 s (8 %) adds 0.03. `ball_state` is `"alive"` on every GS row, so it cannot mark a stoppage.

## 2. Design

### 2.1 What is scored, and in which order

1. **Peaks:** `_candidate_peaks` unchanged (D§4 items 1–2; threshold-free; the rest-run dedup).
2. **Pre-filter:** `min_velocity_change_ms` (default 0.0 with a scorer).
3. **Merge:** unchanged (peaks within `max(merge_window_s, 2 × frame period)` keep the larger |dv|). The merge runs before scoring. At 10 Hz the merge floor equals the spacing of two local maxima, so SkillCorner peaks can chain at threshold 0; that is a known limit (section 3, other providers).
4. **Options:** the **K = 3 nearest players** (horizontal distance at the peak's frame, finite positions). A frame with no player rows gives no options.
5. **Score:** the scorer gives each option the probability that the peak is a real touch by that player.
6. **Best option:** per peak, the option with the highest probability (ties: the nearer rank).
7. **Decoding (new in r7):** a peak keeps its best option only when no other merged peak in the same period within ±`decode_window_s` (inclusive) has a higher best-option probability, and no peak with an equal probability lies earlier in that window. The comparison uses every merged peak that has options, credited or not. A merged peak without options takes no part: it neither suppresses nor is suppressed, and it stays an unresolved row. It is a temporal non-maximum suppression: of the arrival peak and the control peak of one reception, the more probable one survives.
8. **Credit:** a surviving peak is credited when its probability is at least `min_contact_probability` and the ball is not measured above `contact_max_z_m` (2.6 m). Otherwise the peak is an unresolved row (ADR-124 E7 unchanged).

`decide_options(opts, prob, params, *, decode_window_s: float)` remains the one credit gate for the detector and the scorer driver; it applies steps 6–8. `credit_options(opts, prob, params, *, decode_window_s: float)` gains the same keyword. The keyword is required (no default), so every caller states its window. The callers: `_finalize_contacts` (`_ball_contact.py`, passes the loaded scorer's value), `credit_options` (`_contact_scorer.py`), the driver's `_score` (`build_contact_scorer.py`, the fold or refit scorer's value), and the tests that call either function.

**Frame slices.** In scorer mode a credit depends on the frames within about `C` + `decode_window_s` (0.7 s) of the peak, so a caller who passes a slice of a match can get different credits near the slice edges than on the whole match. Every repo caller passes whole matches. The `detect_ball_contacts` and `add_ball_contacts` docstrings state this.

**The decoding window has one source of truth: `model.json`.** The driver writes `decode_window_s` = `DECODE_WINDOW_S` (0.3 s, a module constant of `_contact_scorer.py`) at fit time. At serve time the detector reads only the value in `model.json`; the module constant is the training default, not a serve-time check. `load()` refuses a `model.json` whose `decode_window_s` is missing, not finite or negative (`ContactScorerIntegrityError`); `SHA256SUMS` covers the file. The window is not a feature-contract constant, because it does not change the feature matrix. It is not a `BallContactParams` field.

### 2.2 Features (per peak and option; frames only; provider-neutral; 16 in total)

**The v1 features (9; unchanged except `z`).** The window is the D§4 window `span = max(kinematic_window_s, 2 × frame period)`.

| Feature | Definition |
|---|---|
| `dv` | the peak's |v_after − v_before| (m/s) |
| `speed_in`, `speed_out` | the mean ball speeds over the window before and after the peak (m/s) |
| `turn` | the angle between v_before and v_after (rad, in [0, π]); 0 when either speed is below 1e-9 |
| `dist` | the option's horizontal distance to the ball at the peak's frame (m) |
| `rank` | 0, 1 or 2 |
| `gap` | `min(d_next − dist, 5.0)`, where `d_next` is the distance of the next-nearest player beyond this option; 5.0 when there is none (m) |
| `approach` | the option's distance to the ball at the peak's frame minus its distance at the last frame at or before `t − span` (m; negative = closing); 0 when the player has no such frame |
| `z` | the ball height at the peak's frame when `z_source == "measured"` and finite; otherwise **missing** (NaN) |

**The control features (7; new in r7).** They read the same deduped ball samples as the peak search (ADR-125, `trajectory_dedup_mask(..., rest_s=span)` per period) and the option's frame rows; positions only, no provider velocity column. `C = CONTROL_WINDOW_S = 0.4 s`. Let `k` be the peak's deduped ball sample, at time `t`.

- The **after** samples are `k` and every deduped sample with `t < t_s ≤ t + C`. The **before** samples are every deduped sample with `t − C ≤ t_s ≤ t` (this includes `k`).
- A **distance** is taken at a sample only when the ball position and the option's position at that sample's frame are both finite.
- A **path speed** over a sample set is the sum of the horizontal steps between consecutive samples divided by the elapsed time from the first to the last sample. It is missing when the set has fewer than 2 samples, when the elapsed time is 0, or when any sample in the set has a non-finite ball position.
- A **relative displacement rate** over a sample set uses the first and the last sample of the set at which both the ball and the option are finite: \|(ball_last − ball_first) − (option_last − option_first)\| divided by the elapsed time. It is missing when fewer than 2 such samples exist or the elapsed time is 0.

| Feature | Definition |
|---|---|
| `after_ball_speed` | the path speed over the after samples (m/s) |
| `after_rel_disp` | the relative displacement rate over the after samples (m/s) |
| `after_dist_mean` | the mean distance over the after samples except `k` (m); missing when no such sample has a distance |
| `after_dist_end` | the distance at the last after sample, except `k`, that has a distance (m); missing when there is none |
| `before_dist_min` | the minimum distance over the before samples (m); missing when none has a distance |
| `before_rel_disp` | the relative displacement rate over the before samples (m/s) |
| `speed_drop` | the path speed over the before samples minus `after_ball_speed` (m/s); missing when either is missing |

On the development half the control features separate strongly (medians, labelled options): `before_dist_min` 1.05 m for touches against 5.36 m for the rest, `after_dist_mean` 2.48 m against 6.49 m, `speed_drop` −0.12 m/s against 0.97 m/s.

**Missing values.** The model takes missing values natively (2.3); there is no imputation. This replaces the v1 training-mean reading of a non-measured `z`. In the GS training rows `z` is never missing (GS `z_source` is always `"measured"`, `gradientsports.py:145`), and the control features are present on at least 99.95 % of rows. Their missing-value branches are therefore the estimator's default direction, not learned; a non-measured height on another provider takes that default (the model card says so).

Every feature is rotation- and reflection-invariant (distances, speeds, an unsigned angle), so the scorer has no chirality input (ADR-028 does not apply).

**Frame rate.** The features are defined at every frame rate; their distributions shift at 10 Hz (fewer samples per window). The scorer is trained on GS (29.97 Hz) only. This is a known limit; the D§6 other-provider trigger guards it, and the model card states it.

### 2.3 The model and its artifact

- **Model:** a gradient-boosted tree ensemble, fitted with scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingClassifier(max_iter=200, learning_rate=0.1, max_leaf_nodes=31, min_samples_leaf=20, l2_regularization=0.0, early_stopping=False, random_state=0)` on the 16 features, not tuned. Missing values use the estimator's native missing-value branch. With early stopping off and a fixed seed, two fits on the same rows are equal (a test pins it).
- **Export and serving: numpy only, pickle-free.** `ContactScorer.fit` (the one owner of the export) fits the estimator, then exports each tree to plain arrays (`feature`, `threshold`, `left`, `right`, `missing_left`, `is_leaf`, `value`) plus the baseline raw score. It leaves out every per-sample statistic of the nodes (`count`, `gain`). Serving walks the trees in numpy (the `_FlatTrees` precedent of ADR-076) and applies the logistic link. The export reads the fitted estimator's tree nodes; these are scikit-learn internals, read only at fit time. Two guards: `fit` asserts that the numpy serving equals `predict_proba` on its own training rows (tolerance 1e-12) and raises otherwise, so a scikit-learn release that changes the internals fails at the fit, in the venv that fits; and a CI test asserts the same parity on a toy fit. `metadata.json` records the scikit-learn version of the fit. scikit-learn is already a runtime dependency; serving does not import it.
- **Class:** `ContactScorer` in `silly_kicks/tracking/_contact_scorer.py` (the v1 class is replaced, same name and the same `fit` / `predict_proba` / `to_dict` / `from_dict` / `save` / `load` surface); the error class `ContactScorerIntegrityError(RuntimeError)` (the `PassCompletionIntegrityError` convention).
- **Files:** `silly_kicks/tracking/_contact_scorer_weights/default/`: `model.json` (feature names, the tree arrays, the baseline, `decode_window_s`, the feature contract), `metadata.json` (`training_commit`, `frames_dataset`, `corpus_provenance` with the providers, the 24 development game ids and their count, `stores_training_data: false`, the threshold, the scikit-learn version), `metrics.json`, `MODEL_CARD.md`, `SHA256SUMS` (it must list `model.json`). No per-sample array is stored. The split thresholds are scikit-learn bin edges: at most 255 per feature, taken from a seeded 200,000-row subsample of the 749,688 labelled training rows (quantiles, or midpoints for a feature with few distinct values such as `rank`). They are the same kind of parameter as the shipped xShot and xCross booster JSON. The reversibility test of ADR-116 applies, and a test asserts that `model.json` holds only the declared parameter arrays.
- **Feature contract (ADR-050).** `feature_contract` takes a closure that computes the 2.2 feature matrix on the committed trajectory probe `contact_scorer_probe_frames()` (`_contact_scorer.py`: a deterministic scene with a kick from rest, a deflection and four players, at 25 Hz, 2.2 s). `feature_contract` keeps its keyword-only `probe: pd.DataFrame | None = None` (default: `contract_probe_frame()`, so the existing models are unchanged); `verify_feature_contract` compares the stored and the recomputed contract dicts. An edit to the trajectory probe takes the module's probe-change path (warn, skip the fingerprint). The probe has six merged peaks (1.0, 1.24, 1.36, 1.6, 1.76 and 2.16 s) and its players have rows for its whole 2.2 s. Every control feature is finite on every probe option (the 2.16 s after window holds 2 samples), so the control features enter the fingerprint without a probe change; a test asserts that the probe feature matrix has no missing value. The declared constants, stamped in the contract's `constants`, are the five feature-affecting values: `K_OPTIONS` (3), `GAP_CAP_M` (5.0), `CONTROL_WINDOW_S` (0.4), and the detector fields `kinematic_window_s` and `min_candidate_speed_ms`. None is a geometry constant, so they are not registered in `DECLARED_CONSTANT_SOURCES`: that registry and its enumeration gate (`tests/tracking/test_geometry_constant_enumeration.py`) cover the geometry-named constants of the five listed extractors only. A scorer test pins the set. At `load()`: the constants are compared first (a mismatch raises `ContactScorerIntegrityError`), then the fingerprint; a missing contract warns (`MissingFeatureContractWarning`), the module policy. At serve time, a caller's `kinematic_window_s` or `min_candidate_speed_ms` that differs from the scorer's declared value raises `ValueError`: the features would leave the training distribution.

### 2.4 Labels (development half only)

Unchanged. The PFF on-ball sequences (D§2), period-relative. For each merged peak:
- **near an edge** (within τ = 0.2 s of a sequence's `startTime` or `endTime`; the nearer edge when two are within τ): the option that is that sequence's player is label 1; the other options are 0.
- **in flight** (between two sequences, farther than τ from every edge): every option is 0.
- **inside a sequence**, farther than τ from its edges: not labelled and not used (D§2: the truth cannot judge carries).

### 2.5 Parameters (`BallContactParams`) and outputs

- **`contact_scorer: str | None`.** `"default"` (the bundled weights), a directory path holding the same files, or `None` (the D§4 reach gate). A string keeps the frozen dataclass's `==` and `hash` value-based and keeps `BallContactParams()` free of I/O at import (`_DEFAULT_PARAMS` is built at import). The weights load lazily on first use, cached per resolved path.
- **`min_contact_probability: float`**, used only with a scorer; `__post_init__` checks `0 < value < 1`.
- **`contact_radius_m`** is read only by the reach gate. **`min_velocity_change_ms`** pre-filters the peaks in both modes.
- **Defaults.** Commit 4: `contact_scorer=None`, and the D§4 fields as now (`contact_radius_m` 1.2, `min_velocity_change_ms` 3.0). Commit 5: `contact_scorer="default"`, `min_contact_probability` = the chosen threshold, `min_velocity_change_ms` = 0.0, `contact_radius_m` 1.2.
- **The `None` mode after commit 5** is the model-free reach gate with the shipped fields: radius 1.2 m and no change threshold. Its D§6 tuning found no feasible point, so these values are untuned; the docstring says so, and a caller who opts out sets both. A test pins these defaults.
- **Outputs in scorer mode.** `contact_source`: `"velocity_break"` (credited) or `"unresolved"`; a peak suppressed by the decoding is an unresolved row. `velocity_change_ms`: the peak's |dv|, as now. `confidence`: the scorer's probability for the credited option (in [0, 1]); NaN for an unresolved row. In reach-gate mode the outputs keep their D§4 definitions.
- **Release notice.** The scorer is the default credit step (gradient-boosted, with first-touch decoding); `contact_scorer` and `min_contact_probability` are new; `min_velocity_change_ms` defaults to 0.0; `first_contact_confidence` and `confidence` become the scorer's probability (still in [0, 1]); the `None` mode is untuned; the bar holds on the track-supported pairs, and the memo states the all-pairs figures.
- **Model card, "what it does not measure".** The supported "right" is conditional on the tracks agreeing with PFF (the support filter reads the same receiver-ball distance that `dist`, `before_dist_min` and `after_dist_*` carry). It is not an accuracy for receptions in general. The card states the unsupported share (41 % on the development half) and the all-pairs figures beside the supported ones, and the missing-value default of 2.2.

## 3. Tuning and validation (pre-declared before the held-out look)

- **Split, measures, thresholds:** D§6 (development 24, held-out 40; next touch right ≥ 0.60, false first ≤ 0.10, release timing within ±0.10 s; τ 0.2 s). The measures (`score_next_touch`), `otb_sequences`, the split rule and the selection ordering live in `scripts/_gs_truth.py`, the single source for the materializer, the harness and the scorer driver.
- **The measured population (owner ruling, 2026-11-16).** The "right" and "false first" rows of the bar are computed on the **track-supported pairs**: the pairs S1 → S2 that `score_next_touch` forms, where PFF's S2 player has a finite tracked position within `SUPPORT_RADIUS_M` (1.5 m, horizontal) of a finite ball position at some ball frame with `t2 − τ ≤ t ≤ t2 + τ` (t2 = S2's start). The ball frames are all ball rows, not the deduped samples; a ball row with a non-finite position is skipped; a pair whose S2 player has no finite row in that window is not supported. The release-timing row keeps its D§6 population (every S1 of a kick type). The flag reads PFF's identity and the tracks only; it never enters the labels, the features, the detector or the scorer. It enters only the measures and the threshold selection.
- **Why the population, and what was compared.** The flag removes the pairs where PFF's labels and the tracks disagree on who has the ball (section 1.3), so the bar measures the detector, not that disagreement. It was chosen after the development scores were known. Before the ruling, four populations were scored with the prototype (`supported_pairs.log`; "right" at false first ≤ 0.10): all pairs 0.417; 1.5 m and ±0.3 s 0.635; 2.0 m and ±0.3 s 0.603; 1.5 m and ±0.2 s 0.650. The chosen definition scored best, and the owner ruled with all four in view. The held-out half is still unseen, and the population is fixed here, before the held-out look, so the held-out figure estimates the detector on this population. Every report states the all-pairs measures and the supported share beside the supported ones.
- **`_gs_truth.py` surface (the output contract of the two populations).**
  - `SUPPORT_RADIUS_M = 1.5` (module constant).
  - `track_supported(seq, frames, *, radius_m=SUPPORT_RADIUS_M, tau=TRUTH_TAU_S) -> np.ndarray` (bool, aligned to the rows of `seq` as passed): row i is True when sequence i's player meets the support condition around sequence i's start. The flag depends only on row i. The caller stores it as the bool column `supported` of the sequences table, so it travels with the rows through every `groupby("period_id")`. The first row of a period is the S2 of no pair, so its flag is never read.
  - `score_next_touch(seq, contacts, *, tau=TRUTH_TAU_S)`: unchanged signature and all-pairs counts. When `seq` has the column `supported`, it also returns `sup_n`, `sup_right`, `sup_false_first`, `sup_wrong_player` and `sup_missed`, counted over the pairs whose S2 row has `supported` True. Without the column the `sup_` keys are absent, so every current caller keeps its result.
  - `shares(counts, offsets)`: the current keys keep their all-pairs meaning (`right_share` is the all-pairs share). When the `sup_` counts are present it adds `sup_n`, `sup_right_share`, `sup_false_first_share`, `sup_wrong_player_share`, `sup_missed_share` and `supported_share` (= `sup_n / n`). `timing_median_s` and `release_missing_share` keep the D§6 release population.
  - `meets_bar(m, *, population)` and `select_point(grid, *, prefer, population)`: `population` is a required keyword, `"supported"` or `"all"`. `"supported"` reads `sup_right_share` and `sup_false_first_share` for the two rows (and for the near-best window); `"all"` reads `right_share` and `false_first_share`. The timing row is the same in both. `"supported"` without the `sup_` keys raises `KeyError`.
  - **Who selects on what.** The scorer driver (both modes) and the harness's rule-mode GS grid (`measure_ball_contact_validation.py`, the baseline) select with `population="supported"`, the bar. Both reports carry every grid row's all-pairs and supported shares.
- **Out-of-fold scores on the development half.** 6-fold grouped cross-validation by match: the 24 development ids sorted by the sha256 hex of `str(int(game_id))`, fold = position mod 6. Each fold's scorer is fitted on the other 20 matches; the measures are computed on the out-of-fold credits, with the decoding.
- **The grid:** `min_contact_probability` ∈ {0.05, 0.10, …, 0.95} (19 points). `DECODE_WINDOW_S`, `CONTROL_WINDOW_S`, `SUPPORT_RADIUS_M`, K and the model settings are fixed, not tuned in the run.
- **The selection** (a total order): feasible points (all three rows, out of fold); among them, those within 0.005 of the best "right"; then the larger threshold. No feasible point: the cycle stops before the held-out run and the result goes to the owner. A chosen point on the grid edge goes to the owner before the held-out run.
- **The frozen scorer:** refitted on all 24 development matches, with the chosen threshold. The threshold is chosen on fold models fitted on 20 matches and applied to a refit on 24, so the probability scale can shift slightly; the memo reports the refit's development scores at the chosen threshold beside the out-of-fold ones.
- **The held-out run, once:** the driver's held-out mode scores the frozen scorer on the 40 held-out matches against the bar, on the supported pairs (all-pairs figures beside), at the development threshold. It reads only the driver's development report and the frozen scorer it names, and checks the scorer's `model.json` sha256 against that report; no match list, no other model, a clean tree, the development run's commit only. The development mode reads only the development units of the staged dataset; the held-out mode only the held-out units. A bug found after the look goes to the owner (a fixed rerun is a second look, stated in the memo).
- **Other providers:** the D§6 trigger unchanged (IDSSE and SkillCorner agreement within 0.5 s and the SkillCorner unresolved rate, against the commit-3 v1 reference). It guards the frame-rate shift, the SkillCorner merge chaining and the missing height.
- **The rule baseline is reproducible.** The harness keeps its rule-mode GS truth grid (`--gs-truth`, the development half, `contact_scorer=None`, pining frames) and reports both populations. The runs repeat it on the commit they read, and the memo cites that run. Its frames come from the live pining loader and the scorer's from `gs_contact_truth`, the same loader materialized at commit 4 and proved equal on the detector inputs before the development run (section 4).

## 4. The scorer driver, the harness and the cross driver

Owner ruling 2026-11-15: the scorer is fitted by a campaign-launchable pipeline on staged data (ADR-123: no pining in the job), not inside the pining-reading harness. It is registered as a corpus driver (`DRIVERS`, ADR-128 spec 5.8), not a `REGISTRY` trainer: the `REGISTRY` gates (`tests/scripts/hf_jobs/test_registry.py`, `tests/scripts/hf_jobs/test_headtohead.py`, `tests/scripts/hf_jobs/test_floor.py`) load a committed model and run a same-rows head-to-head against an owner-ratified floor, and a first fit has no committed model to defend. The TF-46 corners models are the precedent for wheel weights fitted by a driver. Moving the scorer into `REGISTRY` is a later step once a committed model exists, the owner's call.

## 8. Non-goals and anti-goals

- **Non-goal:** a sequence model (an HMM or a recurrent network) or a learned decoder. If the 2.3 design misses the bar out of fold, the result goes to the owner.
- **Non-goal:** tuning the model settings, the windows, K, the support radius or the features in the run, or on the held-out half.
- **Anti-goal:** a provider branch; provider-specific weights.
- **Anti-goal:** changing the bar, the measured population or the truth after the held-out look.
